Most people who book their first Botox appointment have been thinking about it for a year or more. By the time they sit in my chair, they have read a hundred opinions and still have the same two questions. Will it hurt, and will I look strange. Here is what the visit actually looks like, so you can decide with facts instead of forum threads.

## Before you arrive

You will get a short intake form the day before. It asks about medications, allergies, previous treatments and anything that would make treatment unsafe, such as pregnancy or certain neuromuscular conditions. If you take blood thinners for a medical reason, do not stop them. Tell us instead, and we will plan around a little extra bruising.

Eat normally. Arrive with a clean face if you can. If you cannot, we will cleanse for you.

## The movement map

The first ten minutes are the most important part of the appointment, and it is the part most people do not expect. I ask you to frown, raise your brows, squint, smile and hold each expression for a moment. I am watching where your skin folds, how strong each muscle is, and whether one side works harder than the other. Almost everyone is asymmetric. Good dosing accounts for that.

Then we talk about what you want. Some people want their frown lines gone and nothing else touched. Some want a smoother forehead but need to keep full brow movement for work. Those are different plans with different unit counts, and you will see the count and the price before I open a vial.

## The injections

Neuromodulator injections use a very fine needle. Each one takes about a second. For a forehead and frown lines, expect somewhere between ten and twenty tiny injections over three or four minutes. Most people describe it as a quick pinch, and many are surprised it is over so soon. Ice or a vibration device is available if you are nervous. There is no numbing cream because it is not needed and it makes the skin harder to mark accurately.

Small bumps appear at each injection point and fade within about thirty minutes. Pinpoint bruising is possible. It is uncommon and fades within a few days.

## The next two weeks

You will walk out looking exactly the same. Neuromodulators take three to seven days to start working, and the full effect settles at two weeks. In the meantime, stay upright for four hours, skip the gym and the sauna for a day, and resist rubbing the area.

At the two-week mark we invite you back for a short review. If one brow sits a little higher, or a line still creases more than you would like, a few additional units balance it. This visit is included. It is also the honest answer to the fear of looking frozen: we dose conservatively on purpose because it is easy to add and impossible to subtract.

## How long it lasts

Three to four months is typical for a first treatment. With regular maintenance, some people find the interval stretches because the muscles are not being trained as hard between visits. Results vary from person to person, and we will never promise a number.
