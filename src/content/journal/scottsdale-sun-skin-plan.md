Scottsdale is one of the sunniest cities in the country. That is wonderful for the golf calendar and difficult for skin. Almost every client I see has some combination of sun spots, freckling on the chest, redness across the cheeks and a general unevenness that makeup has been covering for years. The good news is that most of it is treatable. The catch is timing.

## Why timing matters

Light-based treatments such as IPL work by heating pigment. If your skin is tanned, the treatment cannot tell the difference between a sun spot and your overall color, which raises the risk of burns and blotchy results. That is why I turn away tanned clients, and why I plan most pigment work between October and April.

Summer is not wasted time. It is when we build the habits that make the winter treatments hold.

## Autumn: clear the slate

Once the tan has faded, usually by mid-October, we begin an IPL series. Three sessions spaced a month apart lift brown spots and reduce redness on the face, neck, chest and hands. The spots darken like coffee grounds for a week and flake away. Between sessions, a light peel or a HydraFacial keeps the surface bright.

If texture is also a concern, a light fractional resurfacing session in November gives a fresh surface with a long weekend of pinkness. Deeper resurfacing, for those who want it, is best in December or January when the sun is lowest and social calendars have room for a week of healing.

## Winter and early spring: refine and protect

January through March is prime time for anything that needs the skin to stay out of the sun afterward. Chemical peels, RF microneedling and a final IPL touch-up all fit here. By April we shift to maintenance and home care.

## Summer: protect what you built

From May to September, the plan changes. No IPL, no resurfacing, no medium peels. HydraFacial and gentle light peels are safe year-round and keep pores clear. The real treatment is daily: a mineral sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, reapplied every two hours outdoors, a wide-brimmed hat, and a vitamin C serum in the morning to defend against the damage that gets through.

Melasma, the patchy hormonal pigment across the cheeks and upper lip, deserves its own note. Heat alone can trigger it, so even a shaded patio in July can darken it. If you have melasma, we treat it gently, avoid IPL entirely, and lean on brightening peels and a home regimen instead.

## A sample year

- October to December: IPL series of three, with a light peel between sessions.
- January: RF microneedling or fractional resurfacing for texture.
- February to March: brightening peel, final IPL touch-up.
- April to September: monthly HydraFacial, daily SPF, hats, vitamin C.
- The following October: one maintenance IPL.

Everyone's skin, schedule and tolerance for downtime are different. A consultation lets us map your year rather than a general one. The sun is not going anywhere, and neither are we.
