The most common thing a new patient tells me is a version of this: I do not want to look like I had anything done. I always answer the same way. Good. Neither do I.

Aesthetic medicine has a reputation problem, and it earned it. Overfilled lips and immobile foreheads are visible because they are mistakes. What you never notice is the far larger number of people who look well-rested, well-slept and simply themselves, because their provider knew when to stop. That restraint is the entire craft.

## Structure before surface

Faces change with time in a predictable order. Bone recedes, fat pads shrink and shift, skin thins. Treating only the surface, chasing each fold with filler, produces a face that looks worked on. Restoring a little structure where it was lost, and then leaving the surface alone, produces a face that looks like it aged gently.

That is why every plan here starts with a consultation and a movement map, not a syringe. We look at old photographs when patients have them. The goal is to find what your face used to do, not to invent something new.

## The two-week rule

We dose conservatively on purpose, and we ask you to wait two weeks before judging. Neuromodulators take that long to settle. Filler swelling takes that long to resolve. At the two-week visit we add a few units, or a little more product, if it is needed. It is very easy to add. Taking away is possible with hyaluronic acid fillers, but it is not a plan. Building slowly is.

Patients sometimes leave a first visit thinking we did too little. Two weeks later, nearly all of them are glad we did.

## Saying no

A physician-led clinic should turn people away sometimes. We decline treatment when we do not think it will help, when the request is not in someone's interest, or when a patient's expectations are not achievable. We suggest a different treatment, or none, and we explain why. That conversation is not lost revenue. It is the reason patients trust the yes.

## What natural does not mean

Natural does not mean subtle to the point of invisible. It means proportionate. A patient who wants a stronger jawline can have one, and it will look like a jawline. A patient who wants smoother skin can have it, and it will look like skin. What we will not do is exceed the limits of your anatomy to chase a trend.

Results vary, and no one can promise a specific outcome. What I can promise is a plan built for your face, a provider who would rather do less, and a follow-up visit where we get it right together.
